## 🏗️ **1. CONSTRUCTION & BUILDING MATERIALS**

### **Products:**
- **Roofing materials** (shingles, tar, sealants)
- **Concrete & cement** (ready-mix, bags)
- **Paint & staining products** (exterior)
- **Lumber & treated wood**
- **Asphalt & paving materials**

### **Seasonal Pattern:**
```
Spring/Summer: 250% demand (construction season)
Fall: 120% demand (last push before winter)
Winter: 30% demand (frozen ground, can't build)
```

### **Weather Signals:**
- **Temperature > 50°F** → Concrete can be poured
- **Rainfall < 1 inch/week** → Roofing projects proceed
- **Extended dry period** → Paint/staining surge
- **First frost date** → Last-minute rush

### **Commodities:**
- Petroleum (asphalt, tar)
- Limestone (cement)
- Pigments (paint)
- Treated lumber from mills

### **Example Guidance:**
```
"Temperature staying above 55°F for next 14 days
→ Order 2,500 bags of concrete by March 15
→ Increase roofing shingle inventory at Northeast DCs by 40%
→ High demand expected March 20 - May 15"
```

## 🎯 **WHY THESE ARE PERFECT FITS:**

### **Common Characteristics:**

1. **Weather is a PRIMARY demand driver**
   - Not just correlation, but causation
   - Temperature/precipitation directly affects usage

2. **Narrow seasonal windows**
   - Must capture demand during peak (can't make it up later)
   - Stockout = permanent lost revenue

3. **Commodity procurement**
   - Raw materials have lead times (14-90 days)
   - Prices fluctuate based on supply/demand
   - Must order BEFORE season starts

4. **Multi-location distribution**
   - Different regions = different timing
   - Phoenix spring ≠ Minneapolis spring
   - Weather varies significantly by location

5. **High stockout cost**
   - Customer buys from competitor
   - Lost sale is permanent (season ends)
   - Reputation damage (unreliable supplier)
